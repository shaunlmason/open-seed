# Plan: next exhaustive interleaving check of the append loop and halt (os-07e6e76c)

Implements card os-07e6e76c, the second card of the 2026-09-06
formal-methods survey (`docs/next-build-plan.md` §3, "Borrowed from a
formal-methods survey"). Design authority: SEED-NEXT.md §II.1 (ordering
by admitted ancestry; freshness, the monotonic-head rule, non-fast-forward
refusal on the ledger ref; genesis and halt, "enforced at the admission
boundary, which is what makes it real") and charter III.A row 7 (the race
drill). Builds on the sibling card's instrument (os-21bf939f, #353), which
is sequential by construction: its walker feeds admission one record at a
time and cannot see two writers in flight. This card is the concurrent
half. The tree's evidence about the append protocol today is executions:
the race drill runs two appenders against a bare remote and observes one
real retry (`TestRaceDrillTwoConcurrentAppenders`), the perf gate runs 24
writers, and three drills roll a remote back and watch a client refuse
(`ErrHeadRegression`). Executions show the loop survives the interleavings
the scheduler happened to produce. This task enumerates them.

## Design decisions (binding for this task)

- **D1: a Go-native model and explorer, in the gitref package's tests.**
  `next/internal/gitref/interleave_test.go` carries an abstract state
  machine of the append protocol and a depth-first explorer over every
  interleaving of its enabled steps for small N, with visited-state
  memoization. No second toolchain: the build plan's single-toolchain
  default holds (§1, language and layout), and the state space for the
  sizes below is thousands of states, a couple hundred lines of Go. TLA+
  stays the fallback the card names, reached for only if a later
  configuration outgrows this explorer; if it ever is, the spec lives
  under `next/spec/` with a last-verified stamp and never enters
  `make check-next`. Lean is ruled out for the survey's reason: a proof
  about a model, no extraction to the code that enforces.
- **D2: the model's alphabet is the loop's own steps.** The state is one
  remote (a chain of records, each tagged normal, halt or lift; a posture
  flag, enforced or cooperative) and N writers, each with a draft kind,
  attempts left, a phase, a persisted head and an outcome. Steps, each
  atomic:
  `fetch(w)`, which refuses with the head-regression outcome when the
  remote's tip does not descend from the writer's persisted head and
  otherwise records the tip the writer will build on;
  `attempt(w)`, which validates the draft against the fetched chain
  (a non-lift draft while that chain is halted refuses with the halted
  outcome and the loop returns, as `AppendLoop` returns every
  non-race error), persists the fetched tip, then pushes: it lands
  when the remote's tip is still the fetched tip, appending the record
  with `prev` equal to that tip and persisting the new one, and
  otherwise loses the race (`ErrNonFastForward`), spends an attempt,
  and returns to the fetch phase, or ends with the retries-spent
  outcome at zero;
  `rollback`, the cooperative-posture adversary, at most once per
  run, which truncates the remote to an earlier tip, genesis kept (an
  enforced remote refuses it, the hook's non-fast-forward rule, so the
  step is enabled only in the cooperative configuration);
  the operator's halt and lift are drafts of the writer that carries
  them, not separate steps. The stale-tree refusal (`ErrStaleTree`, a
  hook bad_prev at or beyond the appended position) is a retry the loop
  treats exactly as a lost race; the model folds it into the lost-race
  outcome and says so.
- **D3: the properties, checked at every state and every terminal
  state, each failing with the trace that reached it.**
  (P1) *No lost update*: under an enforced remote, every terminal chain
  is genesis plus exactly the records of the writers whose outcome is
  landed, each once, in landing order.
  (P2) *Prev is the tip at admission*: every landed record's `prev` is
  the tip at the moment it landed; in the model this is the land rule,
  so the property with content is the replay's (D4), where the real
  record's `prev` is compared to the tip the real client fetched.
  (P3) *Monotonic head*: no writer's persisted head ever moves to a
  tip that does not descend from its previous one; after a rollback,
  every writer whose persisted head is beyond the rolled-back tip
  refuses at its next fetch and lands nothing after, and a writer with
  no persisted head accepts the rolled-back tip, which the charter
  names as the fresh-clone residual and this test asserts as such
  rather than hides.
  (P4) *Halt at the boundary*: in every terminal chain, no non-lift
  record follows a halt record before a lift, including for a writer
  that fetched a pre-halt tip: its push loses the race the halt won,
  and its re-validation on the fresh fetch refuses.
  (P5) *Bounded termination*: every writer ends in exactly one of
  landed, retries spent, halted, head regression, and the explorer
  terminates on every path. With one draft per writer, no halt and an
  enforced remote, a writer can lose at most N-1 races, one to each
  rival's single landing, so with attempts A ≥ N no interleaving ends
  in retries spent; the explorer asserts that lemma, and asserts it
  fails at A = N-1 (a counterexample must exist, or the model is not
  racing anything).
- **D4: the model is kept honest by replaying its traces through the
  real client.** `next/internal/gitref/interleave_replay_test.go` takes
  terminal traces from the explorer and drives real clients, one per
  writer, through the loop's own steps against a bare remote in the
  trace's order: `Fetch` for a fetch step, the package's `attempt` for
  an attempt step (the same function `AppendLoop` iterates; in-package
  tests reach it). Every step's outcome class must match the model's
  (landed, `ErrNonFastForward`, a `halt.HaltedError` from re-validation
  under `admit.Validate()`), every landed record's `prev` must be the
  tip that client fetched (P2 for real), and the final chain must
  verify from genesis with the model's records in the model's order.
  The stage is genesis plus the upgrade to seed/1 under one root key;
  writers draft `intent.filed` on distinct subjects with distinct
  timestamps (byte-identical drafts converge on one commit and race
  nothing, the claim-storm drill's finding), and the operator writer
  drafts `system.halt.declared`. The rollback configuration is not
  replayed: three drills in `gitref_test.go` already roll a real remote
  back and assert the refusal, and the model's rollback arm cites them.
  A disagreement between model and client is a finding either way: a
  model error is fixed in the model and recorded; a loop error is a
  D7 fix. This replay is what makes the model evidence about the tree
  rather than about itself.
- **D5: configurations and sizes, the perf-storm split.** The fast
  gate enumerates four configurations: two writers with two attempts
  and three with three, enforced, no halt; two writers plus an operator
  writer carrying a halt, three attempts, enforced; two writers with two
  attempts, cooperative, one rollback. It replays a deterministic sample
  of eight terminal traces drawn across the first and third
  configurations (every k-th trace, k chosen to land on eight), sized
  with the enumeration to stay under roughly five seconds locally on
  Linux and measured on the Windows leg, where every git spawn is dear
  (`next/spec/platform.md`); record both in the test's header comment
  and halve the sample before the configurations if either bound is
  exceeded. The scheduled size, one step appended beside the walker's
  on `.github/workflows/perf-scale.yml`, enumerates four writers with
  four attempts and replays every terminal trace of the two replay
  configurations. Sizes are test flags (`-writers`, `-attempts`,
  `-replay`), passed through `go test ./internal/gitref -args ...`.
  `.github/**` is a protected path: the task PR's review is the
  decision it requires, with #297 and #353 as precedent.
- **D6: outcomes are named by the loop's errors.** The model's outcome
  vocabulary is `ErrNonFastForward`, `ErrRetriesSpent`,
  `ErrHeadRegression` and `halt.HaltedError`, and the replay asserts
  them with `errors.Is` and `errors.As`, so the model cannot drift into
  a vocabulary the loop does not speak. The loop's retryable set (a
  lost race and a stale tree) is pinned the same way: a replayed lost
  race must retry, and any other error must end the writer's run.
- **D7: no production surface changes.** The model, the explorer and
  the replay are test files. If a replay disagrees with the loop and
  the loop is wrong, the fix lands in this task scoped to the smallest
  production change that restores the property, recorded in
  `next/docs/decisions.md` with the trace kept as a named regression in
  the replay; nothing else moves. The verdict-then-merge reconciliation
  and racing mode are the named follow-on, filed once this explorer's
  shape is known, not this card's.

- The explorer enumerates every interleaving of the four fast-gate
  configurations, memoized, and terminates; the state and trace counts
  are logged and recorded in decisions.md.
- P1, P3, P4 and P5 hold on every state and terminal state of every
  configuration where they apply, with a failure naming the trace; the
  A ≥ N lemma holds at A = N and a counterexample is found at A = N-1.
- The replay drives eight sampled traces through real clients over
  `Fetch` and `attempt`, and every step's outcome class, every landed
  record's `prev`, and the final verified chain match the model.
- Outcomes are asserted against the loop's own error values.

  carries the scheduled size.
- `make check` green, coverage gate ≥90% held.
