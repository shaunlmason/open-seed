# os-32d06c65 — revocation reaps the revoked holder's open claims, on the revocation alone

Charter III.E row 8's reap arm ("revocation … reaps its claims"), found
unmet by the Phase 10 exit walk and routed to Phase 12 item 1, whose
compromised-actor drill (plans/os-465e356e.md D9) names this card as its
follow-up: the drill asserts today's consequence (a revoked key's
proposals refuse at the push, its contract branch closes with its
standing), and this card gives the still-open claim its reap. Core
squad, L2.

## What the tree actually shows

- **A reap needs corroboration the ledger can check.**
  `next/spec/maintenance.md` states that silence alone can never reap:
  the maintenance pass reaps a claim only against a boundary-valid
  `run.interrupted` on the active fence (`admit.InterruptRequested` /
  `InterruptValid`) with an `expired`/`wedged` classification, or an
  admitted `wedge.declared` (`admit.WedgeDeclared`). `no_data` — a
  silent stream — carries no reap path at all. The corroboration is one
  shared derivation: `maintain.Corroboration{Interrupted, Wedged}`,
  filled from `internal/admit`, consumed by the pass and enforced when
  `claim.reaped` is admitted.
- **A revoked holder can never act again, and the ledger says so.**
  `keyring` folds `actor.revoked` to `StandingRevoked`; from that
  position on, every proposal by that key refuses at admission (the
  grant and standing rules), and — since #250 — its contract branch
  closes with its standing at the code-ref boundary. But its **claim
  stays open**: nothing emits `claim.reaped`, because there is no
  `run.interrupted` (the revoked worker cannot append one) and no
  `wedge.declared` need have been raised. So the contract sits
  `in_progress` under a key that can do nothing, until someone notices
  — the exact hole III.E row 8 names.
- **The compromised-actor drill already pins the gap.**
  `internal/redteam/testdata/residuals.json` carries the
  `revocation-reap` residual: a revoked key's proposals refuse and its
  branch closes, but its open claim stands until reaped, "carded as
  os-32d06c65". This card closes that residual and extends the drill's
  corpus to assert the reap is now available.

## Design decisions (binding for this task)

**D1. Revocation is a third reap corroboration, beside interrupt and
wedge — and the only one the ledger itself supplies.**
`admit.RevokedHolder(records, table, subject, fence)`: true when the
subject's active claim holder (the `claim.taken` signer at `fence`) has
a **boundary-valid** `actor.revoked` admitted at a position at or before
the reap — judged by the same posture `InterruptValid` uses (the
`actor.revoked` record re-decoded and its signer's standing checked at
its own position, so a raw-pushed revocation corroborates nothing). No
`run.interrupted`, no classification, no wedge: the revocation is
sufficient because a revoked holder provably cannot exit its own window.
Refused: reaping on a revocation of anyone but the fence's holder;
reaping on a suspended (not revoked) holder, whose standing can return.

**D2. `claim.reaped` cites the revocation, and the admission gate admits
on any one corroboration.** The reap payload carries the existing
`{fence, packet}` plus an optional `{revoked: "<position>"}` citation.
The `claim.reaped` admission gate (the reap rule that today consults
`InterruptValid`/`WedgeDeclared`) admits when **Interrupted OR Wedged OR
RevokedHolder** holds for the cited fence; a `revoked` citation that
does not name a boundary-valid revocation of the fence's holder refuses
with a reap-specific reason, never a silent pass. A reap on a **live**
holder still requires the interrupt-or-wedge path exactly as before —
this decision only adds a path, never widens the existing ones. Refused:
a `revoked` citation admitting a reap the two existing corroborations
would not, on a still-active or suspended holder.

**D3. The maintenance pass reaps every open claim a revocation orphans,
with an honest force packet.** `maintain.Corroboration` gains
`Revoked bool`, filled from `admit.RevokedHolder`. The pass, in its
existing reap step, additionally reaps an `in_progress` subject whose
active claim holder is revoked and whose fence carries no `claim.reaped`
yet, emitting `claim.reaped` with the `revoked` citation and a
**force-reap packet** (executors.md's force path): the four parts
present, the findings recording the revocation as the reason and the
loss window honestly (the post-revocation observation gap), the base
range the resume coordinate, so the work is re-offered to the next
worker. Refused: a bare-`{}` reap (silent abandonment) — the packet is
mandatory on every deliberate exit, this one included; a reap that
invents prose in place of the force reason.

**D4. The obligation surfaces the owed reap.** `internal/obligation`
derives, for an `in_progress` subject whose active holder is revoked and
unreaped, that a `claim.reaped` is owed — so `seed situation` and the
maintenance pass see the same debt from one derivation, and a
deployment that runs no maintenance still shows the orphaned claim as
outstanding rather than silently stuck.

**D5. Bounds.** `no_data` still carries no reap path — a revocation is a
ledger fact, not silence, so this decision does not touch the
silence-can-never-reap rule. Suspension is not revocation: a suspended
holder's standing can return, so its claim is not reaped (a drill pins
this). No new capability, transition row or protocol version: the reap
verb, its lane and its fence discipline are unchanged; this adds a
corroboration and a maintenance arm. The redteam residual is updated in
place; the drill's corpus gains one assertion, no ceiling clause moves.

## Acceptance Criteria

**Boundary set (new, shown working):**

1. **Reap on the revocation alone.** A `claim.reaped` citing a
   boundary-valid `actor.revoked` of the fence's holder is admitted with
   no `run.interrupted` and no `wedge.declared`; the contract folds to
   `ready`.
2. **The revocation must be the holder's, valid, and terminal.** A
   `revoked` citation naming a non-holder, a suspended holder, a
   still-active holder, or a raw-pushed revocation refuses with the
   reap's own reason; a reap on a live holder still requires interrupt
   or wedge.
3. **The maintenance pass reaps orphaned claims.** In the fixture, a
   revoked holder's open claim is reaped by one pass with a four-part
   force packet whose findings name the revocation and the honest loss
   window; the contract is re-offered; `no_data` is still never reaped.
4. **The obligation surfaces it.** `seed situation` shows the owed reap
   for a revoked, unreaped holder, from the same derivation the pass
   uses.
5. **The drill's residual closes.** `redteam`'s `revocation-reap`
   residual reads "reap available", and a corpus assertion proves the
   reap is admitted on the revocation alone; the ceiling is unchanged.
6. **Mutation evidence.** Each fails a drill: `RevokedHolder` accepting
   a suspended holder; accepting a non-holder's revocation; accepting a
   raw-pushed revocation; the reap admitted with a `revoked` citation
   naming no valid revocation; the maintenance reap emitting a bare
   packet; the obligation not surfaced.
7. `make check` green with coverage measured cold, three readings above
   the gate; the suites pass unprivileged.

**Retention set (existing, shown unharmed):**

- The interrupt and wedge reap paths behave exactly as before; a reap
  on a live holder is unchanged; `no_data` carries no reap path; the
  silence-can-never-reap rule stands.
- No new capability, transition row or protocol version; every
  pre-existing chain verifies byte for byte; the compromised-actor
  drill's ceiling clauses are unmoved (only the residual text changes);
  projections are byte-identical on chains this card does not create.
