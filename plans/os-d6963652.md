# Plan: next — a reservation outlives its window, so its close must too (os-d6963652)

Closes the hole found while planning `os-52d5da3f` (review finding on
#170): `next/internal/admit/admit.go` gates **all three** budget verbs
on `s.State == "in_progress"`, so a reservation whose claim window
ends can never be settled or released. Both closing verbs refuse with
`invalid_transition`, while `BudgetViewAt` keeps counting the
reservation against capacity.

## The harm is real, and it is not "capacity leaks on done contracts"

Trace each way a window ends:

- **`claim.released` / `claim.parked` / `claim.reaped`** — the subject
  returns to `ready` or `blocked`. Capacity is recoverable *if* the
  original signer claims again, because the budget rule's other
  conditions (reservation exists, valid, unclosed, signer or operator)
  all still hold in a later window. Recovery by luck, not by design.
- **`submission.made` → `review` → a **failing** verdict →
  `contract.returned` → `ready`** — this is the one that bites. The
  next claimant is a **different** worker, who is neither the
  reservation's signer nor the operator, so they cannot close it, and
  their own reserves come out of a `Remaining` silently reduced by the
  previous attempt's unclosed hold. A failed attempt taxes the next
  attempt's budget.
- **`done`** — harmless in capacity terms (nothing spends on a
  finished contract), but the actuals for work that really happened
  can never be recorded, which is the metering row's concern rather
  than the budget's.

So the defect is not cosmetic: it makes a retry after a failed
verdict quietly poorer than a first attempt.

## The card's two options are not alternatives

The card offers (a) an admission change or (b) a maintenance finding
for Phase 9 item 3 to reap. **(b) is unimplementable on its own.**
"Reaping" a stranded reservation means performing some act that frees
the capacity, and with the gate in place *no such act admits* — not
for the worker, not for the operator, not for the maintenance lane,
which is audited as an ordinary actor precisely so it has no private
powers. The card's own note says as much: outside the window "the
advertised dischargers would be unreachable". Detection without an
admissible remedy is a report that nobody can act on.

A third option not on the card — **auto-closing a reservation when its
window ends, in the derivation** — is rejected on the codebase's own
posture. It would record zero spend for work that may have spent
plenty, fabricating a number where none was stated. That is exactly
what the budget rule already refuses to do for unknown classes
("absent knowledge is never fudged into spendable units"), and an
implicit release destroys the distinction between *we spent nothing*
and *nobody said*.

So: **(a)**, with (b)'s detection folded into a surface that already
exists rather than a new lint (D3 below).

## The evidence that (a) is the right shape, not just the available one

`BudgetCloseValid` — the derivation half, landed in Phase 7 — already
validates a close by **identity alone**: the reservation's own
reserving signer, or the operator lane at the attempt's position. It
asks nothing about a window. The derivation was written
window-agnostically and the admission gate was the accidental
over-restriction; the fix aligns admission with a derivation that
already assumed it.

## Design decisions (binding for this task)

- **D1 — the state gate moves from the verb family to the verb.**
  `budget.reserve` keeps `s.State == "in_progress"`: reserving
  capacity for a window you do not hold is exactly what that gate
  exists to prevent, and the holder-or-operator check beside it says
  so. `budget.settle` and `budget.release` **drop it entirely**. No
  replacement gate: a close is legal wherever a valid, unclosed
  reservation of the closer's own stands, because the reservation's
  existence already proves a window once existed, and there is no
  state in which honestly closing one is wrong. Every other condition
  the rule checks (existence, `ReservationValid`, not already closed,
  signer-or-operator, actuals shape) is unchanged.
- **D2 — outside a window a close cites no fence, and the fence rule
  already says so.** "Outside a claim window no fence exists: none is
  required, and citing one refuses." A close inside a *later* window
  must cite that window's active fence, which is also already the
  rule. Nothing in the fence rule changes; both shapes are drilled so
  the pairing is pinned rather than assumed.
- **D3 — the obligation restriction goes away, because its reason
  does.** `os-52d5da3f` emits `budget.open` only inside the live
  window, deliberately: outside it the advertised dischargers were
  unreachable, and an obligation nobody can discharge is an anomaly
  rather than an obligation. Once D1 lands the dischargers **are**
  reachable, so `internal/obligation` drops the `s.State ==
  "in_progress"` condition and an open reservation stands as an
  obligation wherever it is open. That is the detection half of the
  card's option (b), delivered through the projection that already
  exists — no new lint, no build-plan change, and `seed situation`
  surfaces it to the party who can act.
- **D4 — the obligation is owed by the reservation's SIGNER, always.**
  Today the owner is the claim holder when a claim exists and the
  signer otherwise. With D3 that becomes wrong in a way the
  dischargeability sweep will catch: inside a *later* window held by
  someone else, the obligation would be attributed to a holder whom
  admission forbids from closing it. The signer is exactly who may
  close, so the signer is who owes it.
- **D5 — the sweep is the proof, not the drills alone.** The
  dischargeability sweep already walks every prefix of the shared
  scenario asserting that at least one discharging verb admits for the
  owed party. With D3 the sweep necessarily reaches positions where
  the window has ended, so the fix is proven by the class rather than
  by a hand-picked case — the same argument that earned the sweep its
  place in #171.
- **D6 — scope guard.** No new verb, no new state, no new projection,
  no change to `Remaining` arithmetic, and no change to what a settle
  or release *means*. This removes one over-broad condition and one
  restriction that existed only because of it.

## Steps

1. **`internal/admit/admit.go`, budget rule.** Move the `s.State ==
   "in_progress"` check from the shared `isBudget` path into the
   `BudgetReserveVerb` case, leaving the close path ungated on state.
   The spending-verb gate above it is untouched.
2. **`internal/obligation/obligation.go`.** Drop the `s.State ==
   "in_progress"` condition on `KindBudgetOpen` and attribute the row
   to `r.Signer` unconditionally (D3, D4), with the comment rewritten
   to say why the restriction is gone rather than leaving the old
   reasoning stranded.
3. **`next/spec/budgets.md`.** State the settled rule: a reservation
   is opened inside the window that will spend it and closed by its
   own signer or the operator lane **whenever** it is open, with no
   fence cited once the window has ended. Name the failed-verdict
   retry as the case this protects, so the reason survives the change.
4. **`next/spec/obligations.md`.** Replace the "live window only" note
   on `budget.open` with the new rule and its reason.
5. **Drills** (`internal/admit`, `internal/obligation`,
   `cmd/seed`): a reserve inside a window, the window ended by
   `submission.made`, then a settle with **no fence** admitting and
   `Remaining` returning to capacity minus actuals; the same shape
   through `seed budget settle` on the CLI, where the loop verb omits
   the fence key because no window is active (the derivation already
   does this: `activeFence` returns false and the key is dropped); a
   close inside a later window citing that window's fence; a foreign
   closer still refused; a reserve outside a window still refused with
   its existing error; and the `budget.open` obligation standing after
   the window ends with the sweep green across the whole walk.
6. `next/docs/progress.md`, `next/docs/decisions.md`, memory; receipt;
   evidence; review.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. `budget.settle` and `budget.release` admit outside a claim window
   from the reservation's own signer or the operator lane, citing no
   fence; `budget.reserve` still refuses outside `in_progress` with its
   existing message.
2. Capacity returns: after an out-of-window settle, `BudgetViewAt`
   reports the reservation closed and `Remaining` reflects actuals, so
   a subsequent claimant sees the class capacity the class grants.
3. A close inside a later window cites that window's active fence and
   admits; a close citing a fence outside any window still refuses at
   the fence rule.
4. A closer who is neither the reservation's signer nor the operator
   still refuses, in and out of the window alike.
5. `budget.open` stands for as long as the reservation is open, owed by
   the reservation's signer, and the dischargeability sweep is green
   across every prefix of the shared walk — including the positions
   where the window has ended, which is where the defect lived.
6. `next/spec/budgets.md` and `next/spec/obligations.md` state the
   rule and the reason; no verb, state or projection is added.
7. `make check` green, coverage gate ≥90% held.
