# Plan: next III.I random-walk property test over admission (os-21bf939f)

Implements card os-21bf939f, filed from the 2026-09-06 formal-methods
survey (`docs/next-build-plan.md` §3, "Borrowed from a formal-methods
survey"). Design authority: SEED-NEXT.md §II.10 (one rule set, two
consumers, zero drift by construction), charter III.I row 2 (an
affordance-listed verb refused at admission for legality is a bug
class with a regression test), and the §I.2 ceiling as it lands in
admission (`next/internal/redteam/testdata/ceiling.json`, the
charter's clauses as a table). Builds on merged 8.2
(`next/internal/admit/soundness_test.go`, plans/os-148d3ba1.md): the
III.I class is executable today over every prefix of one hand-written
walk (`walkScript`), and every other generator in the tree walks a
scripted shape (`internal/history` drives each contract through the
same ten records; the red-team drill plays hand-picked cases). Rule
interactions at positions no script reaches are tested only where
someone thought of the case. This task adds the instrument that
explores: a seeded random walker over admission, with the III.I class,
the transition rule's refusal, the fold, and the ceiling as its
oracles, and prefix truncation as its shrinker.

## Design decisions (binding for this task)

- **D1: the walker is a test-file harness in the admit package,
  started from the shared walk scenario.** `TestAdmissionRandomWalk`
  in `next/internal/admit/randomwalk_test.go` replays `walkScript`
  through `runWalkStep` (the tree's richest admitted history: every
  lane enrolled and granted, the chain at seed/7, contracts in several
  states), then takes N random steps from that position. Three
  consumers of one scenario, the same shape as the rule set. Contexts
  after the replay come from `ContextOver(records)` on the in-memory
  record slice, never a store: the walker appends nothing to disk, so
  a walk's cost is the fold, and `ContextAt` stays the store seam it is.
  The declaration is nil throughout (policy rules are no-ops by their
  own contract), so squads, agent ceilings and racing mode are outside
  this walk: racing is the seam the sibling card (os-07e6e76c) owns.
- **D2: a step is a draw, not a script.** At each position the walker
  draws, from a `math/rand/v2` source seeded per walk: a lane from the
  six the scenario enrolls (root, holder, supervisor, verifier, sealer,
  observer); a subject from `ctx.Lifecycle.Subjects()` plus `system`
  plus, with probability 1/8, a fresh contract id (so `intent.filed`
  keeps opening new subjects); then one of two moves. With probability
  3/4 the **listed move**: compute `Affordances(ctx, key, subject)`,
  pick a listed verb uniformly, re-draft it independently exactly as
  the sweep does (`probeViewAt` plus the catalog's synthesizer, on the
  subject `probeSubjects` derives where it derives one, signed by the
  lane's key), run the enforcing `Check`, and on admission append the
  record and rebuild the context. With probability 1/4 the
  **forbidden move**: pick a lifecycle verb the transition table
  refuses from the subject's current state (`Table.Allows(from, verb)`
  false), drafted by a lane that holds the verb's capability (the
  scenario's grant map: holder claim, supervisor supervise, verifier
  verdict, sealer sealer, observer observer, root everything), and
  run `Check`, appending nothing. `ledger.UpgradeVerb` is excluded
  from both moves: the scenario already sits at the newest version
  and a downgrade is not a legality question. A listed set that is
  empty for the drawn pair costs the draw and moves on; a walk that
  admits nothing in 32 consecutive draws fails, naming the position,
  because a walker that stops exploring is a broken instrument.
- **D3: four oracles at every step, one at the end, each failing
  with its class named.** (a) *III.I*: a listed verb that `Check`
  refuses fails as the regression class, with seed, walk, position,
  lane, subject, verb and refusal. (b) *Transition*: a forbidden move
  must refuse, and the refusal must unwrap to the transition table's
  error (`transition.InvalidTransitionError`) and not to a later
  rule's; this is the ceiling's `transition` clause ("with a
  capability the actor does hold, so the refusal names the
  transition") as a property over random positions, and refusal
  ordering is what it pins. (c) *Fold*: after an admitted lifecycle
  verb, `ctx.Lifecycle.State(subject).State` equals the `to` that
  `Table.Check(subject, before, verb)` returned for the state before
  the append; after an admitted non-lifecycle verb the state is
  unchanged. (d) *Determinism*: `Affordances` computed twice at the
  position agrees, as the sweep asserts. At the end of every walk,
  `simulate.Audit(records)` must be clean on all five bars, and the
  ceiling coverage table of D4 must hold.
- **D4: the ceiling is covered clause by clause, and coverage is
  asserted from the table, not from memory.** The test loads
  `../redteam/testdata/ceiling.json` through `redteam.LoadCeiling`
  (the package's non-test files import only the standard library, so
  no cycle) and carries a coverage map from every *prohibition*
  clause id to how this walk holds it: `transition` by oracle (b) and
  the audit's chain-violations bar; `spend` by the audit's
  unreserved-spend bar; `lease` by the audit's silent-abandonment bar
  (the walker's deliberate exits are the only exits); `claim` by a
  predicate over the final records (no `claim.taken` admitted on a
  subject while its fold held an active claim, since the declaration
  admits no racing); `approve` by a predicate (every `merge.requested`
  cites a `verdict.rendered` whose signer is not the holder of the
  claim window the submission closed, and whose key held the verdict
  grant at that position); `gates` by a predicate (no keyring verb,
  halt verb, or plan approval signed by any key but root); `rewrite`
  and `impersonate` marked *structural* with the reason: the walker
  signs with the real keys and holds records in memory, so neither a
  forged signature nor a rewritten prefix can arise here, and both
  are the red-team drill's at the hook. A clause the map does not
  name fails the test, so a clause added to the table fails here until
  someone says how the walk holds it. Predicates are written beside
  the map in the test file; if implementation finds the same
  predicate already derivable from `simulate` or `reconcile`, reuse it
  and say so in decisions.md.
- **D5: replayable, and shrunk by construction.** Every walk's seed
  is derived from a base seed and the walk index and logged with
  `t.Logf` on failure; the walker's draft clock is fixed (the
  scenario's last timestamp plus one second per step) so a seed
  replays byte for byte, which means `probeViewAt` takes its `now`
  from a parameter in this task rather than reading the wall clock
  (test-helper change; the sweep passes what it passed before). A
  failure is positioned, so the shortest failing prefix is the
  minimal case: the failure message carries the step script up to the
  failing position (one line per step: lane, move, verb, subject,
  admitted or refused), which is the shrunk reproduction. No shrinking
  library: `testing/quick` is too weak and rapid would be a fifth
  module dependency for something prefix truncation gives for free.
- **D6: two sizes, the perf-storm split.** Under `make check-next`
  the walker runs 8 walks of 48 steps, sized to stay under roughly
  five seconds locally (the sweep's own bound); record the measured
  time in the test's header comment, and halve the steps before the
  walks if it is exceeded. The counts are test flags (`-walks`,
  `-steps`, `-seed`, declared in the test file and passed through
  `go test ./internal/admit -args ...`). The scheduled size, 200 walks
  of 96 steps, is one step appended to
  `.github/workflows/perf-scale.yml` beside the storm, on the same
  weekly schedule and read-only identity, attaching nothing (a red
  run is the signal and the log carries the seed). `.github/**` is a
  protected path: the task PR's human review is the decision that
  path requires, and the precedent is #297, which added the workflow
  itself from a `next:` card.
- **D7: no production surface changes.** The walker is tests and
  test helpers. If a walk finds a real divergence (a refusal-order
  bug, a fold that disagrees with the table, a probe that lists what
  the rules refuse), the fix lands in this task scoped to the smallest
  production change that restores the property, with the seed and the
  shrunk script recorded in decisions.md and the fixing walk kept as a
  named regression seed in the test. Nothing else moves; the
  interleaving check is the sibling card's.

## Acceptance Criteria

- The walker replays the shared scenario and takes the configured
  number of random steps per walk, drawing lanes, subjects and moves
  per D2, and never stalls (32 admitted-nothing draws fail by name).
- At every step, a listed verb that admission refuses fails as the
  III.I class; a forbidden move refuses with the transition table's
  own error; the fold's state after an admission matches the table's
  `to`; affordances are deterministic. At the end of every walk the
  five-bar audit is clean.
- Every prohibition clause in the ceiling table is named in the
  coverage map, and each predicate the map cites holds over the final
  records of every walk; an unnamed clause fails the test.
- A failure reports the seed and the step script to the failing
  position, and rerunning with `-seed` reproduces it.
- The fast gate runs the D6 size inside the measured bound;
  `perf-scale.yml` carries the scheduled size.
- `make check` green, coverage gate ≥90% held.
