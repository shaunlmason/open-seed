Filed from the same formal-methods survey (2026-09-06) as its sibling, the admission random walk. That card is sequential by construction and cannot see interleavings; this card is the concurrent half. Charter sections extended: SEED-NEXT.md II.1 (freshness, the monotonic-head rule, non-fast-forward refusal on the ledger ref), II.1 genesis and halt (halt enforced at the admission boundary, not by the writer's client), and III.A row 7's race drill. No Part III row changes; not schedulable ahead of an open Phase 13 row.

WHAT EXISTS. internal/gitref/loop.go AppendLoop: fetch, materialize, verify, re-link, re-sign, push, with ErrStaleTree and ErrNonFastForward retried up to maxAttempts and ErrHeadRegression refused before any attempt (gitref.go Fetch, the monotonic-head rule). internal/halt StateAt and Check. The race drill in internal/gitref/gitref_test.go runs two concurrent appenders against a bare remote and observes a real retry. The perf gate runs 24 writers. These are executions, not enumerations: they show the loop survives the interleavings the scheduler happened to produce.

THE GAP. No test enumerates interleavings. The properties the charter states about the append protocol are interleaving properties: no lost update; every admitted record's prev was the remote tip at the moment of admission; a client's persisted verified head never regresses across any sequence of fetches, refusals and retries; a writer holding a pre-halt tip is refused at the boundary whatever its client does; the loop terminates under bounded attempts with fair scheduling and reports the typed remote rejection when it does not. For three writers and three attempts the space is small enough to walk exhaustively, and a walk is evidence a two-appender run is not.

WHAT THIS CARD OWES. An abstract state machine of the append protocol and a checker that enumerates every interleaving for small N, asserting the five properties above as invariants and producing the offending trace as the failure message. Actors: N writers, one remote holding a ref with fast-forward-only semantics, one halt-capable operator. Steps are the loop's own: fetch (with head persistence and the regression refusal), attempt (validate against the fetched tip, relink on stale tree), push (refused unless fast-forward), and the operator's halt.declared and halt.lifted. The model treats admission as a yes/no oracle; the rules inside it are the sibling card's.

TOOLCHAIN, DECIDED PER THE PLAN'S RUBRIC. Binding default: Go-native. Write the state machine and an exhaustive depth-first explorer in Go, in the next module, under make check-next at small N. This keeps the build plan's single-toolchain default (docs/next-build-plan.md section 1, language and layout) and the explorer for this state space is a couple hundred lines. TLA+ with TLC is the alternative if the model outgrows that: if the plan reaches for it, the spec lives under next/spec/ with a last-verified stamp (charter II.15), runs on the scheduled job or by hand, and never enters make check-next as a second toolchain. Lean 4 is ruled out for the reason in the survey: a proof about a model, no extraction to the code that enforces.

The plan decides: N and attempt bounds under the fast gate versus the scheduled run; whether the model's initial states are seeded from the sibling's generator (yes, if that card has merged; otherwise a fixed preamble and a follow-up to reuse it); how the model is kept honest against loop.go (candidates: the model's step names cite the loop's error values and a test asserts the set of retryable errors matches; or the drill in gitref_test.go replays a model trace against the real remote and expects the same outcome).
