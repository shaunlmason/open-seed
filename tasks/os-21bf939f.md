Filed from a formal-methods survey (2026-09-06) that asked whether Lean 4 belongs in Seed and answered no: a proof would be about a model of internal/admit, never about the Go rule set that enforces. What the survey found worth building instead is this card and its sibling (the interleaving check). Charter section extended: SEED-NEXT.md III.I (affordances and admission share one rule set) and the section I.2 ceiling as it lands in admission. No Part III row changes; this card may not be scheduled ahead of an open Phase 13 row (docs/next-build-plan.md section 3, filable-now rule).

WHAT EXISTS. internal/admit/soundness_test.go makes the III.I regression class executable: at every prefix of one hand-written walk (walkScript in affordances_test.go), for every enrolled lane, the listed set is sorted and deterministic and every listed verb admits when independently re-drafted. internal/history generates a seeded, byte-deterministic chain, but every contract follows the same ten-record happy path (RecordsPerContract). internal/redteam loads the section I.2 ceiling as a clause table from the charter's own words and drills it clause by clause against the real hook. internal/simulate/audit.go judges any chain against five bars and returns evidence lists, never a boolean.

THE GAP. Every generator walks a scripted shape. Rule interactions at positions no script reaches are tested only where someone thought of the case: park then reclaim under halt, an escalation raised while a request is unanswered, a verdict on a returned contract, racing mode with a reap mid-flight, a budget refusal at a spending gate inside a race. admit.go is about 2,500 lines with some thirty refusal kinds, and refusal ordering is where position-stamped refusals have already needed review fixes (#84, #93, #98). Nothing explores the state space.

WHAT THIS CARD OWES. A seeded random walker in internal/admit that starts from a generated preamble and, at each position, picks an enrolled lane at random, reads its affordances at that position, and drafts either a listed verb or, some declared fraction of the time, a verb the transition table (next/spec/transitions.json) forbids at the subject's current state. It runs the enforcing Check and folds the result. Four oracles:
1. A listed verb admits (the III.I class over random positions, not one script).
2. A table-forbidden verb refuses with the transition rule's reason, not a later rule's (refusal ordering is the property).
3. The fold's state after each admission equals the table's `to` for that verb.
4. At the end of the walk the chain passes simulate.Audit clean, and the redteam clause table read as predicates over the chain holds: no done without a key-disjoint verdict, no fence out of order, no spend outside a reservation, no self-approval.

Shrinking is prefix truncation: a failure is positioned, so the minimal case is the shortest failing prefix. No shrinking library; testing/quick is too weak and rapid would be a new dependency on a module that has four. The seed is logged on failure so a red run replays.

Budget follows the perf-storm pattern: a small iteration count under make check-next (state the wall-clock budget in the plan), the large count on the scheduled run.

The plan decides: where the randomized generator lives (a Shape or random mode on history.Spec, which already carries Seed, is the obvious seam, and the sibling card reuses it); the forbidden-verb fraction; which lanes the walker plays (all six, or work lanes only with the operator verbs scripted); and whether the ceiling predicates are derived from redteam's Clause table or written beside it (derive, unless the table's vocabulary does not name a predicate).

NOT THIS CARD. Concurrency. The walker feeds one event at a time; two writers in flight is the sibling card's, which inherits this card's generator and predicates.
